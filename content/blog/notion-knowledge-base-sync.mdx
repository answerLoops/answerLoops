Most teams don't have a documentation problem. They have a Notion
workspace with three years of setup guides, changelogs, and
runbooks in it, and a support inbox that keeps answering the same
questions those pages already cover. The docs exist. They just
aren't where the question is being asked.

Notion pages also go stale in a specific, quiet way: a page ranks
fine in Notion's own search a year after the process it describes
changed, someone follows it, something breaks, and what should have
been a five-minute doc fix shows up as a support ticket instead.
Nobody notices until a customer hits the gap.

## What gets imported

In answerLoops, Notion is a knowledge base source, not a chat
channel — it doesn't produce tickets, it feeds the content the AI
drafts answers from, alongside URL crawls, file uploads, and a
GitHub repo sync.

Setup has two separate steps, and both matter:

1. **Create a connection.** In Notion's developer portal, you create
   an access token scoped to read content only — no update or insert
   capability, no user information. Paste that token into
   **Integrations → Notion** in answerLoops.
2. **Share pages with it.** A fresh connection can see nothing by
   default. You open each top-level page or section you want synced
   and add the connection under **••• → Connections**. Sharing a
   parent page shares everything nested under it, so this is usually
   a handful of clicks, not one per page.

That second step is the part people miss, and it's also the point:
there's no page picker inside answerLoops deciding what's in scope.
You decide, in Notion, by what you share. Nothing syncs beyond that.

## Syncing and what happens to old content

**Sync now** on the Knowledge Base page walks every shared page and
database, converts it to text, chunks it, and embeds it — the same
pipeline every other source runs through. It queues as a background
job now instead of running inside the button click, which used to
time out on anything past a small workspace.

A re-sync **replaces** the previous Notion import rather than
merging into it. Unshare a page and re-sync, and it drops out of the
knowledge base. The replacement only swaps in once the new import
finishes building successfully — an interrupted sync (a dropped
connection, a revoked token) leaves the last good import in place
instead of half-overwriting it.

Formatting conversion is pragmatic, not exact: nested numbered
lists, multi-column layouts, and embeds don't carry over perfectly,
and images and files get dropped (captions survive). If a page's
meaning lives in a diagram or a table, that's worth a plain-text
summary in Notion — the chunker only needs readable prose to work
with.

## Why it stays unpublished until you say so

Every other knowledge base source in answerLoops goes live the
moment it's imported. Notion doesn't, and that's deliberate: a
workspace built for internal writing has drafts, half-finished
pages, and notes-to-self mixed in with the pages that are actually
ready for a customer to read.

After a sync, Notion content shows up as searchable from the
dashboard — your team can already query it — but it isn't served to
the widget or used in customer-facing answers until someone clicks
**Publish to widget** on the Notion panel. Review what imported, fix
what's wrong, then flip it on. That choice is preserved across
future re-syncs, so publishing isn't something you redo every time
someone edits a page.

## The actual value

This isn't about writing more documentation. It's about connecting
the documentation that already exists — the pages a team already
maintains for itself — to the support channels where customers are
asking, and keeping that connection current instead of letting a
one-time import quietly drift out of date. Every re-sync is a
chance to catch what changed; every unpublished-until-reviewed
import is a chance to catch what shouldn't have been there in the
first place.
