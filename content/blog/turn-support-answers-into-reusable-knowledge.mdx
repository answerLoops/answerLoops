Every support team has answers living in the wrong place: a fix buried in a
Discord thread, a workaround in a GitHub comment, a setup detail only one
person remembers. The question gets resolved, the answer stays hidden, and the
next person who asks starts from scratch.

A knowledge base solves that, but only if saving an answer takes less effort
than writing it again. Some answers are already in your docs. Others come out
of a support conversation or are things someone on the team just knows. Your
team, and any AI agent helping it, needs a straightforward way to save those
answers, review them, and keep them current.

This post walks through how
[answerLoops](https://github.com/answerLoops/answerLoops), the open-source AI
support tool I'm building in public, handles that: writing an answer by hand,
importing docs you already have, promoting resolved tickets, reviewing
unanswered questions, and checking that a customer question actually finds the
article.

I started it from my own work answering community questions in Discord,
GitHub, and Slack at CopilotKit, then added integrations for more platforms so
other teams could use it too.

[Explore answerLoops on ⭐ GitHub](https://github.com/answerLoops/answerLoops)

## Answering the question is only part of the work

A question comes in. You find the relevant documentation or resource, figure
out how it applies to that person's setup, and write a reply.

During that conversation, you might discover something the docs don't cover.
A missing setup step, a confusing default, or a workaround that deserves its
own article.

You resolve the question, but that useful detail stays buried in the
conversation. When someone else asks, the team has to find it again.

I want answerLoops to help with both parts: preparing a useful answer now and
saving what the team learns for future questions.

## What answerLoops does

![Ticket and website chat workflows in answerLoops](/blog/turn-support-answers-into-reusable-knowledge/chat-workflows.png)

answerLoops is an open-source AI support tool that brings questions from
Discord, Slack, GitHub, Telegram, and other connected channels into one
dashboard. It uses your knowledge base to help prepare answers.

For knowledge-based replies in the ticket pipeline, a Mastra agent generates a
draft and a separate agent scores its confidence and whether it fully answers
the question. Automatic replies are off by default. When enabled, answers that
meet the configured criteria can be sent automatically; other drafts remain
available for human review.

There's also an embeddable website chat widget built with CopilotKit and
connected to a Mastra support agent. Visitors can ask questions directly, with
published knowledge-base articles supplied as reference material.

The widget supports richer responses, including step-by-step guides, quick
replies, callouts, link cards, and contact options.

But the quality of those answers depends on the information available to the
agent. That's why much of the recent work has been on the knowledge base.

## Add the answer that isn't in your docs

![Entering a question and answer, then publishing it directly to the knowledge base](/blog/turn-support-answers-into-reusable-knowledge/write-an-answer.gif)

Sometimes you don't need to import a documentation site. You just need to
write down something the agent should know.

An owner or admin can open **Knowledge Base → Manage** and use **Write an
answer by hand**:

1. Enter a question or title.
2. Write the answer or context.
3. Click **Add to knowledge base**.

For example, a customer might ask whether changing a configuration requires a
restart. Someone on your team knows the answer, but it isn't documented
anywhere.

The article is published and indexed when it's saved, making it available for
subsequent questions. There's no draft step in this form, so the answer should
be suitable for customers to read.

Adding an article doesn't retrain the model. It gives the agent another
reference to retrieve when a relevant question comes in.

Articles added this way appear under **Added by hand** in the Knowledge
coverage tab.

It's a small feature, but it gives the person with the answer a direct way to
improve what the agent knows.

## Bring in the knowledge you already have

![Knowledge sources including documentation, files, GitHub, Notion, manual answers, and resolved tickets](/blog/turn-support-answers-into-reusable-knowledge/knowledge-sources.png)

You can also populate the knowledge base from existing content:

- **Documentation URLs:** import a page or crawl a documentation site.
- **Files:** upload PDF, DOCX, Markdown, text, or CSV files.
- **GitHub:** sync Markdown and MDX files, with optional syncs when changes
  are pushed to the default branch.
- **Notion:** connect pages and databases your team wants to use.

Notion content imports unpublished. Your team can inspect it before making it
available for customer-facing answers, and that publishing choice is preserved
across syncs.

The manual-entry form sits alongside these sources. If an answer already
exists in your docs, import it. If it only exists in someone's head, write it
down.

## Turn a resolved ticket into reusable knowledge

Closing a ticket helps the person who asked. Turning that resolution into a
knowledge-base article makes the explanation available for future questions.

The **Promote to KB** action lets an owner or admin turn that ticket's
question and answer into a knowledge-base article.

Before promoting it, check that the answer applies beyond the original
conversation. Remove account-specific details and make the explanation useful
to someone encountering the same problem later.

This matters for the website widget in particular. Its retrieved reference
material comes from published KB articles, rather than raw resolved tickets.

Promoting an answer gives it a place in the shared knowledge base where the
widget can find it.

## Let customer questions help you decide what to write

You won't know every gap in your documentation ahead of time.

The **Review** tab captures questions that weren't covered by the available
knowledge. On hosted plans, this queue is available on Pro and above.

A usable draft can come along as a starting point. If there isn't one, the
item is marked **No usable answer**, and someone can write it.

Owners and admins can edit the question and answer, then choose **Approve and
add to knowledge base**. If the item shouldn't become an article, they can
reject it instead.

Nothing in the queue becomes searchable customer-facing knowledge until it's
approved.

When the same question comes in again, its counter increases instead of
creating another copy. The most-asked questions appear first.

If several people ask about the same setup step, that's a useful signal about
what your knowledge base is missing. The queue gives your team a question to
work from and a place to review the answer before publishing it.

## Go from a knowledge gap to a new article

![The review queue and Knowledge Gaps provide separate paths to adding published articles](/blog/turn-support-answers-into-reusable-knowledge/review-queue-and-gaps.png)

The **Knowledge Gaps** page provides another view of where support needs
attention.

It surfaces tickets with low-confidence answers, tickets that need a person,
and resolved questions without a covering KB article.

Repeated questions are grouped with an **Asked N times** badge, so you can see
recurring topics without reading every ticket separately.

The **+ KB** action opens the manual-entry form with the ticket's question and
any existing answer filled in. Review the text, add the article, and it's
linked back to the ticket to close that gap.

You don't have to leave the question behind and start a blank document
somewhere else.

## Check what the agent can actually find

![Knowledge coverage shows article availability and lets you test the widget's retrieval](/blog/turn-support-answers-into-reusable-knowledge/knowledge-coverage.png)

Importing a document doesn't tell you whether it will help with a particular
question.

The **Knowledge coverage** tab shows articles grouped by source, including
whether they're live, unpublished, or not searchable.

There's also a **Try a customer question** field. It runs the same lookup the
website widget uses and shows which articles match, or that nothing covers the
question.

After adding an answer, try the original customer question and a few
variations. Does the lookup find the article you just wrote? If it doesn't,
you have something concrete to investigate before judging the generated reply.

This checks retrieval, not whether every generated reply will be correct. You
still need to review the answers themselves. But it gives you a way to
investigate whether the intended information is being found.

The KB is the source of product knowledge your team maintains for the agent.
If no article matches, the assistant can fall back to general knowledge, which
may not fit your product. Finding those gaps and adding the right material is
part of maintaining it.

## See which articles are being used

![Article usage tracks references supplied for generated answers, excluding searches and simulations](/blog/turn-support-answers-into-reusable-knowledge/article-usage.png)

answerLoops also tracks article usage.

It counts when an article is supplied as context for an answer that is
actually produced, including website replies and ticket drafts.

The distinction matters: this means the article was given to the agent, not
necessarily that the final reply quoted it.

Searches and simulations don't increase the count.

Together with the coverage view and repeated-question counts, this helps you
understand what information is being retrieved and where more work might be
useful.

## Where CopilotKit and Mastra fit

I work in DevRel at CopilotKit, so part of building answerLoops is putting the
tools I work with into a product and sharing what I learn.

Mastra runs the agents behind the support drafts and website conversation,
including the chat agent's thread memory.

CopilotKit provides the foundation for the custom widget interface. The
application connects that interface to the agent and supplies the relevant
knowledge-base context.

The product also has its own workflows around those pieces: importing sources,
reviewing unanswered questions, publishing articles, and handling staff
actions on tickets.

Those workflows are where the team gets to improve the information the agent
uses. Someone can spot a missing answer, write it, publish it, and check
whether a future question retrieves it.

That's become a much bigger part of answerLoops as I've kept building.
